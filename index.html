Pad 39-A · 28.573° N · Five flight plans

Max Q

A solar system on real ephemerides — the Sun, Mercury, Venus, Earth, the Moon, Mars and Jupiter, each on its own Keplerian orbit with its true mass, radius, spin and axial tilt. Earth carries its real coastlines, bathymetry, deserts, mountain ranges, ice sheets and city lights; every launch pad stands where it really stands, most of them on a shore, with a Gerstner-wave sea running up the beach. A WGS-84 Earth with J2 and J4, the pull of the Sun, Moon and planets on every trajectory, the 1976 Standard Atmosphere with jet streams and Dryden turbulence, sphere-of-influence handovers and Lambert targeting; a cockpit with a crew of four, a satellite to deploy, and a sandbox for the road to Alpha Centauri. A scattering sky, the true night sky of five thousand catalogued stars and the Milky Way, and engines that throw a turbulent three-dimensional plume no two ignitions of which are alike.
